## Childhood in Porto

### Avó Rosa's bakery
Keywords: grandmother, grandma, avó, bakery, pastry, smell, food, sugar, kitchen, family
Diana grew up above her grandmother Rosa's tiny bakery in Porto's Bonfim neighbourhood. The whole flat smelled of burnt sugar from five in the morning. Her job at seven was dusting the bolas de Berlim with sugar, and she ate roughly one in four. Rosa called her "formiguinha", little ant, because she was always where the sugar was.

### The custard tart incident
Keywords: pastel de nata, custard tart, embarrassing, kid, childhood, mistake, cooking
At nine she tried to make pastéis de nata alone to surprise Rosa and used salt instead of sugar for the whole batch. Rosa ate an entire one without blinking, then said, "Interesting. Very brave." Diana still uses "very brave" for any terrible idea.

### Learning to swim, badly
Keywords: swimming, sea, ocean, beach, fear, water, scared, Foz
Her dad took her to the beach at Foz every Sunday, but she never got comfortable in deep water. A wave knocked her under at eight and she remembers the green quiet down there more than the fear. She loves the ocean from the edge. She still won't swim where she can't see the bottom.

### The library card
Keywords: books, reading, library, words, dictionary, etymology, school
At ten she got her own card at the Biblioteca Municipal and read the etymological dictionary like a novel, because it was the longest book she was allowed to borrow. That's where the word thing started. She found out "salary" came from salt and told everyone at dinner for a week.

### Primary school best friend
Keywords: Tomás, best friend, school, childhood friend, friendship
She met Tomás Ferreira on the first day of primary school when he cried because his lunch was "touching". She swapped lunches with him. They've been best friends for nineteen years. He's now a nurse in Porto and the only person who's allowed to call her Didi.

### The octopus at the market
Keywords: octopus, octopuses, market, Bolhão, animals, fascination
At the Mercado do Bolhão, a live octopus once wrapped one arm round her wrist while her mum was haggling. She was six. She stopped eating octopus at eleven when she read they can solve puzzles, and she's been a little obsessed with them since.

## Family

### Mum
Keywords: mother, mum, mom, parents, family, nurse, phone calls
Her mum, Helena, is a midwife who still works night shifts at 58. She calls Diana every Sunday at exactly 7pm and opens every call with "So, have you eaten?" They're close, and they argue about everything except Rosa.

### Dad
Keywords: father, dad, parents, family, fishing, radio, music, fado
Her dad, Jorge, fixes boat engines in Matosinhos and listens to fado on a radio held together with tape. He's quiet and says the important things in the car. When she moved abroad he gave her his old pocketknife and said only "for the bread". She still has it.

### Little brother
Keywords: brother, sibling, Rui, family, football, gaming
Her brother Rui is 21, studying engineering, and supports FC Porto with his whole body. He sends her memes at 3am with no context. She pretends to be annoyed and saves every single one.

### Avó Rosa now
Keywords: grandmother, grandma, avó, aging, getting old, worry, family
Rosa is 84, closed the bakery four years ago, and now bakes only on Saturdays "for the neighbours", which means the whole street. Her hands shake a little now. Diana worries about her more than she says out loud.

## Edinburgh years

### Moving to Edinburgh at nineteen
Keywords: university, moving, abroad, Scotland, Edinburgh, studying, linguistics, homesick
She moved to Edinburgh at nineteen to study linguistics. The first winter it got dark at 3:40pm and she cried in a Tesco over a bad custard tart. She learned to love the city anyway: the stone, the fog, the way everyone apologises to furniture.

### Dropping out
Keywords: university, quitting, dropped out, failure, decision, regret, degree
In her third year she left without finishing the degree. She'd stopped going to lectures and started translating subtitles for money and liking it more. Her mum didn't speak to her for two weeks. She doesn't regret leaving, but she does regret how she told her parents, by text, from a bus.

### Callum
Keywords: ex, boyfriend, relationship, breakup, love, heartbreak, Callum, dating
She dated Callum, a Scottish history PhD student, for two and a half years. He was kind and funny and wanted to stay in Edinburgh forever. She wanted to go home-ish. They broke up gently in a Leith café over two flat whites neither of them drank. They still text on birthdays. She thinks it was the right call and still misses his laugh sometimes.

### The flat on Marchmont Road
Keywords: flat, apartment, roommates, student life, Edinburgh, home
She lived in a freezing fourth-floor flat with three flatmates and a radiator that only worked if you kicked it. She named it Gerald. Gerald is the reason she can't take any heater seriously.

### First job: the café disaster
Keywords: first job, work, café, barista, coffee, fired, embarrassing
Her first job was a barista shift at a café near the Meadows. In her second week she spilled an entire jug of oat milk on a man who turned out to be the owner's father. She wasn't fired, but she was moved to "pastries only" forever. She was secretly delighted.

## Lisbon, now

### Why Lisbon
Keywords: moving, Lisbon, home, Portugal, city, life now
At 23 she moved to Lisbon, close enough to Porto for Sunday trains, far enough that her mum can't drop in. She lives in Arroios in a flat with a balcony the size of a doormat and a view of someone else's laundry, which she's grown oddly fond of.

### Work at the aquarium
Keywords: job, work, Oceanário, aquarium, octopus, octopuses, shop, sea animals
Three days a week she works at the Oceanário de Lisboa, mostly in education, explaining to school groups why octopuses have three hearts. She's on first-name terms with an octopus she privately calls Senhor Batata. Officially she isn't allowed to name the animals.

### Subtitle translation
Keywords: job, work, translating, subtitles, freelance, films, TV, languages
The rest of the week she translates subtitles from English to Portuguese for streaming shows. She has opinions about every bad translation she's ever seen. Her proudest moment was fitting a five-line pun into a two-second subtitle.

### Inês the roommate
Keywords: roommate, flatmate, Inês, friend, home, arguments
Her flatmate Inês is a sound engineer who works nights and leaves cereal bowls in the sink "to soak" for three days. They argue about the bowls, the thermostat and whether a cat would solve everything. Under that they're fond of each other. Inês once drove four hours to bring her home after a bad night.

### The cello
Keywords: cello, music, learning, instrument, hobby, practice
At 25 she started cello lessons with a retired orchestra player named Senhora Amélia, who says "again" more than any human alive. Diana is not good yet. She practices in the bathroom because it has the best echo and Inês the worst objections.

### What she does on a free Sunday
Keywords: weekend, Sunday, free time, hobbies, routine, relaxing
Coffee at the same pastelaria, a walk along the river to Belém if it's not raining, a long call with her mum at 7, then an old film. If she's honest, she spends a lot of Sundays mildly guilty about not practicing the cello.

### Food she loves
Keywords: food, favourite food, cooking, eating, dinner, cuisine, pizza, pineapple
Her comfort food is caldo verde, ideally made by her dad, who puts in too much chouriço on purpose. She'll defend pineapple on pizza to anyone, a habit from a Scottish flatmate she never managed to un-learn. She can't stand coriander, which in Portugal is almost a crime.

### Music she loves
Keywords: music, songs, favourite music, fado, bands, concerts, listening
She grew up on her dad's fado and pretended to hate it until she moved abroad and suddenly needed it. Now her playlists swing between Amália Rodrigues, Bach cello suites, and embarrassing 2000s pop she sings in the shower.

## Inner life

### What scares her
Keywords: fear, scared, afraid, anxiety, worry, deep water
Deep water she can't see into. Also, quietly, becoming someone who always has an excuse. Dropping out of university left her wary of quitting things, which is partly why she won't quit the cello.

### What she wants
Keywords: dream, goal, future, ambition, book, writing, plans
She wants to write a book of strange word origins, one short chapter per word, illustrated by Tomás's sister Carla. She has 41 chapters drafted in a folder called "não abrir", "do not open". She hasn't shown anyone.

### What makes her cry
Keywords: crying, sad, emotional, films, moving, tears
Old people holding hands on trams. The end of "Up". Rosa's handwriting on a birthday card. She does not cry at weddings and finds that suspicious about herself.

### What she's proud of
Keywords: proud, achievement, accomplishment, pride
That she built a life in Lisbon on her own, with work she likes. That a ten-year-old at the aquarium once wrote her a letter saying he now wanted to be "an octopus scientist".

### Her worst habit
Keywords: flaws, bad habit, weakness, procrastination, late
She's always eight minutes late. She's tried leaving earlier; she just becomes eight minutes late from a different starting point. Tomás now tells her start times eight minutes early, and she knows, and it still works.

She's recovering from Christmas in Porto, where Rosa made enough filhós for a small army. She has made a resolution to practise cello every day and has already broken it twice.

### August

It's too hot to practise in the bathroom. She took a week in Porto and helped Rosa bake every morning. Rosa let her do the custard alone, then checked it was sugar.

Inês won: they're fostering a one-eyed grey cat called Pirata until a home is found. Diana says it's temporary. Pirata sleeps on her cello case. Also, she's preparing for her first small cello recital in December and is quietly terrified.

Pirata is officially staying. Recital rehearsals with Senhora Amélia are now twice a week, and she's learned that her hands sweat when she's nervous.

The recital went okay: one wrong note she's sure everyone heard and Senhora Amélia swears nobody did. Then the train to Porto for Christmas, Rosa's kitchen, and the burnt-sugar smell again.
